The Scale of the Issue
Community land is not marginal — it is the majority of Kenya. Yet it remains the least protected, least registered, and most vulnerable to dispossession.

The Law & The Promise
Kenya made landmark constitutional and legislative commitments to community land rights. A decade on, the gap between the law on paper and reality on the ground remains vast.
  • 📜
    Constitution of Kenya (2010), Article 63 — formally recognized community land as a distinct, protected category of land ownership for the first time. → Read
  • 📋
    Community Land Act No. 27 of 2016 — enacted to operationalize Article 63. Gave communities a legal pathway to register, own, and self-govern their land collectively. → Read
  • ⏱️
    The Act required the transition process to begin within 12 months of gazettement of its regulations. That deadline passed years ago with no national enforcement.
  • 🏛️
    The Act promised communities the right to form Community Land Management Committees (CLMCs), adopt bylaws, and receive formal title deeds.
  • 🔧
    NLC (Amendment) Act, 2025 — strengthens NLC oversight and requires review of all public land grants issued before 27 August 2010, with power to revoke irregular allocations. → NLC
  • 🚨
    10 years on: the law exists. The land does not yet belong to the people it was written for.

The national average transition rate, standing at 15% as of July 2023, is slow — seven years after the enactment of the Community Land Act.

— NLC-Namati Joint Monitoring Report, October 2023
Sources: Community Land Act 2016 · NLC-Namati Report 2023 · NLC Launch Statement

Top Barriers to Registration

  • ⚡
    Internal conflicts among group members — cited in 39.7% of cases. 50% of studied ranches experienced active conflict during the process.
  • 💸
    Financial constraints — communities cannot afford survey, adjudication, and legal costs. Cited in 22.5% of cases.
  • 📢
    Lack of information on the transition process — cited in 20.6% of cases.
  • 🗂️
    Registration procedural challenges — bureaucratic bottlenecks and delays — cited in 17.2% of cases.

The Gender Crisis Within the Crisis
Kenya's Constitution and the Community Land Act both guarantee women's land rights. Neither is being implemented. The exclusion of women is happening invisibly — and in some places, deliberately.

  • 📜
    The law is clear: Constitution Articles 27 & 60 guarantee gender equality in land. The Community Land Act mandates at least one-third women representation in all CLMCs.
  • 🚫
    Reality: A Kenya Land Alliance audit found that none of the community land inventories submitted by counties contain gender-disaggregated data — making it impossible to even measure women's participation. → Daily Nation
  • 🚫
    In Laikipia's Ilipolei community, women have reported being physically barred from community assembly meetings — the very meetings that determine land governance. → Report
  • 📅
    A National Women Land Conference is planned for 2026, alongside calls for a presidential directive on women's land rights. → ILC Africa

New Threats in the Decade of Delay
Every year of delayed registration opens new frontiers of dispossession. Carbon markets, extractives, and conservation projects are racing ahead of community rights.

Carbon Markets

  • 🌿
    Kenya's vast unregistered rangelands are being targeted for carbon offset projects — without community consent or defined legal rights to carbon revenues.
  • ⚖️
    January 2025: The Environment and Land Court in Isiolo ruled in Osman & 164 Others v Northern Rangelands Trust that conservancies were established without adequate community participation on unregistered community land. → Namati
  • 📋
    Kenya's new climate regulations are still being drafted and do not yet define carbon rights — leaving communities legally exposed to having carbon revenues extracted without consent.

Extractives & Infrastructure

  • 🛢️
    Large-scale investment in ASAL regions — LAPSSET corridor, oil in Turkana, conservation projects — are proceeding on unregistered community land. The Nakukulas case shows communities can lose a decade of resource benefits without a title. → Natural Justice
  • 🏗️
    Unregistered community land has no legal protection against alienation. County governments acting as trustees have a documented history of disposing of community land irregularly.

Conservation Capture

  • 🦁
    September 2025: The High Court revoked the Kamuthe Wildlife Conservancy licence (76,602 acres) in Garissa, finding it was established without adequate public participation — even though Kamuthe was Kenya's first titled trust land. The community land title itself remains valid. → Daily Nation → Eastleigh Voice

Wrong Laws Being Applied

  • 📁
    In counties like Isiolo — where over 90% of land is former trust land — the Land Adjudication Act (Cap 284) has been improperly invoked, creating legal disputes and protests. The correct law is the Community Land Act 2016.
Sources: Namati May 2025 · Daily Nation Sept 2025 · Eastleigh Voice Sept 2025 · Natural Justice 2025
Case Study — Nakukulas Community, Turkana
The most documented of the 4 confirmed trust land registrations. It took 8 years. The community sits on an oilfield. And the battle did not end with the title deed.
Featured Case · Turkana East Constituency · #4 of 4 Confirmed Trust Land Titles
Nakukulas — The First Title Deed in an Oilfield
Nakukulas is a pastoral community in Turkana East whose ancestral land sits in the South Lokichar Basin — the same land where Tullow Oil discovered oil at the Ngamia-1 well in 2012. For over a decade, oil was extracted from their land while they had no legal standing to claim compensation, negotiate terms, or demand a share of revenues. Their land was held in trust by the Turkana County Government — not by them.

Registration began in 2017. It included: inventory publication, CLMC elections, community registration, bylaw development, adjudication, and final registration. The community was gazetted in 2023 as one of 63 village units in Turkana. The title deed was issued on February 27, 2025. The formal celebration was held on May 1, 2025 in Nakukulas village. Supported by Natural Justice, Turkana Extractives Consortium, Kenya Oil and Gas Working Group, and Pamoja Trust.

What Changed With the Title

  • ✅
    The community is now legally recognized as both an entity and an owner. The County Government's trustee role has legally ceased.
  • ✅
    In cases of compulsory land acquisition, the community will now be compensated directly — not through the county government.
  • ✅
    The community can now negotiate directly with Tullow Oil's buyer (Gulf Energy Limited) as a recognized landowner.

What the Title Did Not Resolve

  • ⚠️
    A 2025 court case — TIPD v Turkana County Government — reveals the county government refuses to relinquish trustee powers even after title was issued. → Kenya Law
  • ⚠️
    Lease revenues paid by an investor are allegedly being channelled into county budgets rather than to the Nakukulas and Kapese communities. → KEELC 7179
  • ⚠️
    Neighbouring communities Kasurio and Lomunyenkurat still only hold Form CLA-4 — not a title deed. They remain in the same queue Nakukulas occupied for 8 years.

Graves of our relatives — our primary evidence of land ownership — were not enough to negotiate compensation for land used for petroleum activities.

— James Emuria, Nakukulas Resident · Daily Nation, May 2025
Sources: Natural Justice March 2025 · Daily Nation May 2025 · TIPD v Turkana County KEELC 7179 (2025)
Government Position
Senior officials have acknowledged the failure publicly. Commitments have been made. Structural resourcing and political will to close the gap remain absent.
  • 🏛️
    PS for Lands Nixon Korir at the October 2023 NLC-Namati report launch: acknowledged the process as "slow and inadequately resourced" and committed to channelling resources toward implementing the Community Land Act. → NLC
  • 🏛️
    NLC Chairperson Gershom Otachi: said 14% "shows we are doing something but should do more" — calling for targeted sensitisation and adequate financial and human resource allocation to achieve 100% transition. → Talk Africa
  • 📋
    NLC (Amendment) Act, 2025: Strengthens NLC powers and requires review of all public land grants issued before 27 August 2010 — with powers to revoke irregular allocations. → NLC
  • ✊
    Community response — November 2024: The Community Land Owners Association of Kenya (CLOAK) staged a protest march in Nairobi, presenting a petition to the National Assembly demanding MPs act on registration delays.

14% shows we are doing something but should do more. We need targeted, all-inclusive sensitisation and adequate financial and human resource allocation to achieve 100% transition.

— NLC Chairperson Gershom Otachi · October 2023

Campaign Demands
Seven concrete asks for the Community Land @10 campaign — grounded in verified data and actionable within Kenya's existing legal and policy framework.
1
Register all 315 group ranches by 2027
Set a binding national deadline with quarterly public reporting on progress by county.
2
All 24 counties must submit inventories
Immediately — with mandatory gender-disaggregated data in every submission.
3
Deploy dedicated land registrars to ASAL counties
Communities cannot be expected to travel to neighbouring counties for services.
4
Define carbon rights in law — now
Before any new carbon project proceeds on unregistered community land.
5
Enforce one-third women's representation
In all CLMCs — with independent verification mechanisms, not self-reporting.
6
Adequately fund community land registration
The full cost of registration cannot continue to fall on communities with zero resources.
7
Publish a live national register
Of all community land applications, statuses, and title deeds issued — publicly accessible, updated quarterly.
